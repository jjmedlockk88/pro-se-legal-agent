# Criminal Defense Skill — Nye County, Nevada

## Intake checklist

Collect, without guessing: court, case number, charges/statutes, custody status, next hearing, arraignment/plea status, counsel status, discovery received, warrants/conditions, relevant dates, and current deadlines. Preserve the criminal docket and every filing.

## Workflows

### 1. Docket and deadline audit

Create a chronological table: event, source, date/time, rule/order potentially controlling, deadline, proof of service, status, and consequence. Verify all dates against the official docket and applicable current Nevada/federal rules.

### 2. Discovery and disclosure audit

Index police reports, body camera, dispatch/CAD, 911, photographs, lab records, warrants, returns, affidavits, interviews, impeachment material, and chain-of-custody records. Separate missing evidence from evidence that merely appears unfavorable. Analyze Brady/Giglio issues only after identifying materiality and disclosure history.

### 3. Suppression / dismissal issue spotting

Map each search, seizure, stop, detention, interrogation, identification, warrant, consent, and evidence transfer to facts and proof. Do not assert a violation without identifying the government action, legal rule, disputed fact, and requested remedy. Draft a hearing-question list and exhibit list.

### 4. Plea and sentencing support

Never direct the user to accept/reject a plea. Build a comparison sheet of charged exposure, plea terms, collateral consequences, factual basis, waivers, sentencing ranges (verified), defenses, and questions for appointed counsel or a licensed attorney.

### 5. Appellate/preservation audit

Track objections, offers of proof, rulings, written orders, transcript requests, notice deadlines, and issues preserved or forfeited. Flag uncertainty and obtain current official rules.

## Required warning

If the user is represented or has appointed counsel, emphasize that communications may be privileged and that the user should coordinate through counsel. Civil discovery and public statements can affect the criminal case; flag that conflict before proceeding.
