# §1983 / Monell Skill — Federal District of Nevada / Ninth Circuit

This is an issue-spotting and proof-building workflow, not a promise that a claim is viable.

## Claim map

For each defendant and count, record:

1. Person/entity and capacity.
2. State action.
3. Constitutional right and clearly defined deprivation.
4. Specific act/omission, date, location, and actor.
5. Causation and injury.
6. Remedy sought.
7. Immunity, limitations, exhaustion, standing, jurisdiction, abstention, Heck-related concerns, and other procedural risks.

## Monell proof matrix

A municipality is not liable merely because it employs a wrongdoer or because an incident occurred. Build separate evidence columns for:

- Written policy or official decision.
- Established custom/practice shown by sufficiently comparable incidents.
- Final policymaker decision or ratification.
- Failure to train/supervise, including notice and deliberate-indifference proof.
- Direct causal link / moving force.
- Constitutional injury and damages.

For every theory, list the exact documents, testimony, admissions, prior incidents, training materials, audit findings, or official decisions needed. Do not label a pattern `custom` until comparability, frequency, notice, and causal connection are analyzed.

## Litigation workflow

1. Preserve the criminal record and identify overlap with the civil claims.
2. Build a defendant-capacity and entity chart.
3. Research current controlling Supreme Court and Ninth Circuit authority from primary sources.
4. Draft a fact chronology before drafting counts.
5. Run a Rule 12(b)(6) opposition simulation and eliminate conclusions.
6. Check limitations and accrual claim-by-claim.
7. Analyze whether success would imply invalidity of a conviction or sentence and flag Heck/abstention issues.
8. Prepare narrow, proportional discovery tied to each element.
9. Maintain a service and docket calendar.

## Discovery targets

Subject to the court's rules and orders: policies in force on the incident date, training curricula and attendance, body-camera retention logs, CAD/dispatch, complaints and dispositions, internal investigations, discipline/ratification records, audits, prior notice, budgets or staffing records only when causally relevant, and records identifying the final policymaker.

## Opposition simulation

For each count, write the strongest defense argument for: no constitutional violation, no personal participation, no policy/custom, no notice, no causation, immunity, limitations, improper defendant, lack of jurisdiction, pleading deficiency, and damages bar. Then list admissible evidence—not rhetoric—that answers it.
